FACTOR LAB — prompts for generated media
==========================================

The app's look, for reference: an "analysis workstation" frame (dark slate / light
grey), one amber accent (#E39B2D), and qgraph colours for data (blue #0000D5 for
positive, red #BF0000 for negative). Path diagrams use white circles for latent
factors and white squares for measured items, without Greek letters. The current
key art (cover.png) is a glowing round-bottom flask in which two factors rise as
bubbles above six correlated items; the image prompts below use the same idea so
the results are directly comparable.


------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. BACKGROUND MUSIC  (Gemini / Lyria)          -> save as assets/bgm.mp3
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Instrumental background music loop for an educational web game about structural
equation modeling and factor analysis, used in graduate courses and workshops. The
player studies correlation plots and draws statistical models, so the music must
support focused thinking and never compete for attention.

Mood: calm, curious, quietly optimistic, a little playful, like late-evening work
in a cosy research lab with computers humming. Not sleepy, not dramatic.
Style: minimal modern electronica with a light lo-fi warmth. Soft analog synth
pads, a gentle plucked or mallet arpeggio (marimba / kalimba-like), a round
warm bass, sparse brushed or muted electronic percussion, and a few very quiet
"computer" blips and soft bubbly sounds as texture (a nod to a laboratory and
a running analysis).
Tempo: about 84 BPM, steady. Key: a warm major or Dorian mode, consistent
throughout.
Structure: one even groove with small variations; no intro build-up, no drops,
no big climaxes, no fade-out. It must loop seamlessly: same energy and
instrumentation at the start and at the end. Length 60-90 seconds.
Mix: soft and mellow, low-mid focused, no harsh highs or sharp transients; it
will play quietly underneath short sound effects.
No vocals, no lyrics, no spoken words.

2. SQUARE TILE / COVER  (ChatGPT Image, 1:1, 1024 x 1024)  -> cover.png
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Square cover illustration for an educational web app called "Factor Lab", about
factor analysis and structural equation modeling.

Title: at the top, "FACTOR LAB" in large, clean, bold geometric sans-serif capitals
(the word "LAB" in warm amber, "FACTOR" in off-white), with a small letter-spaced
line "STRUCTURAL EQUATION MODELING" above it. Spell every word exactly like this.

Main subject: a glowing round-bottom laboratory flask resting on a cork ring on a
dark lab bench, centred in the lower two-thirds. The flask is lit from inside, so
the glass reads as a bright white figure against the dark background. The lower
half holds a pale cream liquid. Six small white squares float in the liquid in
two groups of three, each group joined by bold royal-blue lines. Above the liquid
surface, two larger white spheres (the hidden "latent factors") rise like bubbles.
Each sphere has three bold royal-blue arrows pointing down into its three squares.
A curved, softly blue double-headed arrow arches between the two spheres. A few
tiny bubbles rise up the neck of the flask and out of the top.

Setting: a quiet research lab at night. Deep slate-navy background with a faint
graph-paper / screen grid, a softly out-of-focus computer monitor in the far
background showing a blue-and-red correlation heatmap and a network graph, and a
warm amber glow around the flask.

Style: polished, modern digital illustration, semi-realistic glass with soft
lighting and depth, clean shapes, crisp diagram lines. Grown-up and elegant
rather than cartoonish, suitable for graduate-level teaching. Palette: deep slate
and navy, white glass, cream liquid, saturated royal blue (#0000D5) for the
diagram, a touch of muted red, amber (#E39B2D) accents.

Avoid: any other text, numbers or formulas, Greek letters, extra arrows or
labels, people, logos, watermarks, borders. Keep the title and the flask inside
the central 85% of the image so nothing important touches the edges.
